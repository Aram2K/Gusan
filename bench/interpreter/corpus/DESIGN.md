# Interpreter benchmark corpus

Ten short simulated calls for testing the Gusan Interpreter (live speech-to-speech translation)
and meeting transcription. ElevenLabs voices read every line; `generate.py` renders them into
mic / system / mix tracks (plus the speakers and noisy variants) and a `timeline.json`.

The JSON is the ground truth: `text` is what was said (the speech-to-text reference) and `ref`
is what a good interpreter says (the translation reference). Check any edit with
`python bench/interpreter/selftest_corpus.py`; `--update-design` rewrites the table below.

## Conventions

- **Sides.** `me` is the person using Gusan: their lines are the mic track, everyone else's are
  the system (call) track. Every line of `me` is in `my_lang`, every other line in `their_lang`.
- **lang / langs.** `lang` is the line's main language; `langs` lists every language in it, main
  first. More than one = a mixed line. A Latin-script word inside a non-Latin line counts (Zoom in
  Arabic, "Tom" in Chinese): speech-to-text has to write it in Latin letters and the voice has to
  say it the English way. The self-test checks the letters of every line against its `langs`.
- **ref.** Their lines have `ref[my_lang]`, mine have `ref[their_lang]`, and every line has
  `ref.en` (an English line's `ref.en` is the line itself). References are faithful and natural:
  they drop fillers, keep names, numbers and addresses, and write read-out emails as addresses
  ("billing собака nordsoft точка dev" -> billing@nordsoft.dev).
- **Numbers.** Digits where the voices read them reliably (en, ru, fr, es, ar, ja, zh). In Armenian
  and Georgian lines numbers are written as words, because a voice may read digits there in
  English; the references use digits. Compare numbers after normalising words and digits.
- **Emails and URLs** are written as spoken ("r dot moss at northpeak dot io"). Speech-to-text may
  return either form, so normalise "@"/"." and "at"/"dot" (also собака/точка, arobase/point,
  arroba/punto, 艾特/点) before scoring.
- **gap_before** is the silence between the end of the previous line (in list order) and the start
  of this one. Negative = overlap, never below -0.6 s and never on the first line.
- **Voices.** Only the 9 premade voices of `voices.json`. Speakers never use Sarah or Eric, the
  app's default hear voices, so a corpus voice is never Gusan's output voice (this also keeps the
  speakers variant fair: the mic's own voice differs from the leaked translation). River (neutral)
  is unused because the hear voice is matched on gender. No voice repeats within a conversation.
  The voices keep their British or American timbre in every language; `accent_note` says who the
  person is.
- **notes** are free text with #tags that `report.py` can group by: #question #short #filler
  #mixed #numbers #money #date #time #email #url #spelled #names #glossary #formal #casual
  #monologue #long #overlap #pause #gender. The self-test keeps them true (#overlap = negative gap,
  #pause = gap of 2 s or more, #mixed = more than one language, #monologue = 25-35 s).
- **tags** describe a conversation. `speakers` and `noisy` ask `generate.py` for those variants.
  Tags that follow from the turns (1-to-1, 3-person, man-and-woman, two-women, mixed, overlap,
  pause, numbers, money, dates, time, email, url, spelled, glossary, fillers, short-replies,
  monologue, long-turn) must match them; standup, client-call, formal, casual and fast are free.

## Characters and budget

The month has about 20,000 ElevenLabs characters for generating the corpus and for the runs.
Generating the corpus costs its grand total (above) once: `corpus/cache` keeps every clip, so
re-rendering the mixes and variants is free. A run that voices every translation costs about the
total length of the references (above), which is more than the corpus itself. So:

- score translation quality and translation speed on text, without voicing;
- voice only a sample to measure the voice step (for example the hear side of c01 and c02), always
  under `--max-chars`;
- the bench counts characters, not credits; if the plan bills a model at a lower credit rate,
  the same characters cost less.

## Expected weak spots to look at

- **Armenian and Georgian corpus audio.** Use a model that speaks them (eleven_v3 or
  eleven_v4_turbo; eleven_multilingual_v2 has neither) and listen to c01, c02, c04 and c10 once
  (or round-trip them through speech-to-text) before trusting their scores.
- **Gender agreement.** The interpret prompt does not know who is speaking. c03, c06 and c07 show
  whether it guesses right; passing the speaker's gender (the pitch guess on the hear side, the
  person's own profile on the speak side) would fix it.
- **Backchannels and fillers** ("Mm-hm", "Ըհը", "um"): drop them or voice them fast; they must
  never hold up the next phrase.
- **Same-track overlaps** (c01, c02, c07): the pitch-based voice choice during two voices at once.
- **c01 speakers variant:** the leaked English translation is in Aram's own language, so the speak
  side must not translate it back into the call (the echo-loop worst case).
- **Long pauses:** the phrase before the pause must go out at once, not wait for the next words.
- **Read-out emails:** the translation should give the address, not "собака" in English.
